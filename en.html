Hospital cleanliness and environmental hygiene

How to clean each area. How frequently to clean. How to mix sodium hypochlorite. How to care for mops, washrooms and drains.

40 minutes · 1.1 Principles · 1.2 Procedures · 1.3 Logistics and equipment

Module 1 · 1.1 PrinciplesGuidebook p. 37

Why we clean: the chain of infection

  • An infection occurs only when all six links join.
  • If you remove one link, the chain breaks.
  • Link 2: Your cleaning removes the places where germs live.
  • Link 4: Clean hands stop germs that move from person to person.
Module 1 · 1.1 PrinciplesGuidebook p. 34

High-touch and low-touch surfaces

High-touch surfaces

Many hands touch these. Germs move from them to patients.

Door handles · lift buttons · bed rails · light switches · telephones · keyboards · bedside tables · monitors · the wall around the toilet · edges of curtains near patients

  • Clean high-touch surfaces more frequently than other surfaces.

Low-touch surfaces

Few hands touch these. The risk is lower.

Floors · walls · ceilings · mirrors · window sills

  • Clean low-touch surfaces on the schedule.
  • If a surface is visibly dirty, clean it immediately.
  • Flat (horizontal) surfaces collect more dust and germs than walls. Clean them more frequently.
Module 1 · 1.1 PrinciplesGuidebook p. 35–36

Each hospital area has a risk zone

High risk

OT · ICU · HDU · Casualty · labour room · dialysis unit · CSSD · post-operative ward · surgical ward

  • Clean frequently and fully. Use a high-level disinfectant.
Moderate risk

General wards · laboratory · blood bank · pharmacy · kitchen · laundry · mortuary

  • Clean regularly. Between rounds, clean dirty spots.
Low risk

Corridors · offices · stores · record room · seminar room · public areas

  • Clean regularly. Use detergent or a low-level disinfectant.
  • A toilet, staff room or office next to an area has the same risk zone as that area.
  • The risk zone can change. During an outbreak of diarrhoea or flu, a ward becomes a higher-risk area. Obey the new instructions from your supervisor.

Module 1 · 1.2 ProceduresGuidebook p. 40

Before you clean and while you clean

Before you start

  1. Look for an isolation sign at the door. Obey the precautions on it.
  2. Get all the cleaning materials before you go into the room.
  3. Make sure that your equipment is clean.
  4. Prepare the solution. Then clean your hands and put on gloves.
  5. Remove clutter from the area.

While you clean

  • Remove visible dirt before you apply the disinfectant.
  • Use a dry dust mop before the wet mop.
  • Dip the mop one time. Clean 120 sq ft (approximately 11 m²). Then dip it again.
  • Replace the solution after 240 sq ft, or immediately after blood or body fluid.
  • Do not shake mops. Do not use a broom in patient care areas.
  • Look for needles and sharp objects before you mop.
Module 1 · 1.2 ProceduresGuidebook p. 48, Figure 19

Use the three-bucket system

Bucket 1

Detergent water

Dip the mop. Clean the floor.

Bucket 2

Clean water

Rinse the dirty mop. Squeeze it in the wringer.

Bucket 3

Disinfectant solution

Dip the mop. Clean the floor again.

  • Use light-coloured buckets. You can see dirty water quickly.
  • Do not put a dirty mop back into bucket 1 or bucket 3 before you rinse it in bucket 2.
  • Replace the water in bucket 2 when it becomes dirty.

For bleach with a minimum of 5% sodium hypochlorite. Each line makes 1 litre.

SolutionUseBleachWater

Module 1 · 1.2 ProceduresGuidebook p. 42–47

Other cleaning chemicals

Detergent or liquid soap

General cleaning. With good scrubbing, it removes 80–90% of germs.

  • Mix it just before use. Discard what is left.

Aldehyde-based disinfectant

High-level disinfectant for OT, ICU and labour room surfaces.

  • Daily use: 7 ml in 1 litre of water.

Alcohol 60–90%

Small items only: thermometers, stethoscopes, equipment buttons.

  • It burns easily. Do not use it on large surfaces or near a flame.
  • Keep all chemicals in a locked cupboard, in a cool place, away from sunlight.
  • Use the container with the earliest expiry date first.
  • Do not pour chemicals into the drain without treatment. Do not use phenyl in the nursery or on food surfaces.

Module 1 · 1.3 LogisticsGuidebook p. 39–40, 52, 59, 75

Washrooms, drains and pest control

Washrooms

  • Clean toilets with chlorine solution. It also removes bad smell.
  • Clean the wall around the toilet. Many hands touch it.
  • Use a rubber wiper for the floor. Keep the floor dry.
  • Make sure that there are water, soap and a bin.

Drains

  • Keep all drains covered.
  • Clean drains one time each week.
  • If a drain is blocked or leaks, tell your supervisor.
  • Do not let water collect. Still water lets mosquitoes and flies breed.

Pests

  • Remove cobwebs and bird nests.
  • Keep bins closed. Remove food waste every day.
  • Do not let stray animals into the hospital.
  • If you see rats or cockroaches, tell your supervisor.

Put each waste in the correct colour

YellowBin with yellow bag
  • Body parts (anatomical waste)
  • Soiled cotton and dressings
  • Expired or discarded medicines
  • Chemical waste
RedBin with red bag
  • Contaminated plastic
  • IV sets and tubing
  • Catheters, urine bags
  • Syringes without needles
  • Gloves

  • Ampoules and medicine vials

  • Not infectious
  • Food waste, paper, packaging

Module 2 · 2.2 HandlingGuidebook p. 55–58 · BMW Rules 2018 amendment

Fill, tie, label and bar-code the bags

  1. Replace the bag when it is 3/4 full.
  2. Hold the bag at the top. Tie it tightly.
  3. Put a label on the bag: biohazard symbol, date and area (ward).
  4. Put the bar-code label on each bag and container that goes to the common treatment facility.
  5. Put a new bag of the same colour in the bin.
  • Do not fill a bag more than 3/4. A full bag can tear or spill.
  • Do not push down on waste with your hands. Sharps can injure you.
  • Do not use chlorinated plastic bags.
  • The label and bar code show where the waste came from.
Module 2 · 2.2 HandlingGuidebook p. 57–59

Move waste safely inside the hospital

  • Collect waste every day at fixed times.
  • Use a closed, covered trolley with wheels and a biohazard label.
  • Use a different trolley, at a different time, for general waste.
  • Use the route on the hospital route map.
  • After each trip, wash, disinfect and dry the trolley.
  • Do not put general waste in the central BMW store.
Module 2 · 2.2 Staff safetyGuidebook p. 58, 77, 121–122

Protect the health of waste handlers

Wear this PPE when you handle waste

  • Gum boots
  • Heavy-duty gloves
  • Mask
  • Eye protection
  • Plastic apron
  • Get all doses of the Hepatitis B vaccine. Get the tetanus vaccine.
  • Go for your health check-up each year.
  • The hospital keeps a record of your vaccines and check-ups.
  • Go to the training on waste management. The hospital keeps a training record.
  • If you get an injury from waste, do first aid and tell your supervisor immediately.

Spill management

Blood, body fluids and chemicals. Act quickly. Protect yourself first.

40 minutes · 3.1 Blood and body fluid spills · 3.2 Spill kit · Chemical and mercury spills

Module 3 · 3.1 Blood and body fluidsGuidebook p. 112

Small spill: a few drops, less than 10 ml

  1. Put on utility (heavy-duty) gloves and other PPE.
  2. If there are sharps or glass, pick them up with forceps. Put them in a puncture-proof container.
  3. Wipe the spill with newspaper or absorbent paper wet with 1% sodium hypochlorite.
  4. Discard the paper in the yellow bag. Do this again until no soil is visible.
  5. Mop the area with 1% sodium hypochlorite. Let it dry naturally.
  6. Remove the gloves. Clean your hands.
1% · 500 ppm

10 ml of 5% bleach + 990 ml of water.

  • Do not pick up glass or sharps with your hands.
  • Do not dry the area with a cloth. Let it dry naturally.
Module 3 · 3.1 Blood and body fluidsGuidebook p. 112

Large spill: more than 10 ml

  1. Close off the area. Keep people away.
  2. Put on utility gloves, apron, mask and shoe covers.
  3. Cover the spill with absorbent paper, pads or cloth. This stops the spill from spreading.
  4. Pour 10% sodium hypochlorite on it. Make the spill and paper fully wet. (Or put chlorine granules on the spill, then cover it.)
  5. Wait 5 minutes (contact time).
  6. Remove the paper. Discard it in the yellow biohazard bag.
  7. Wipe again with 10% solution until no soil is visible. Mop one time. Let it dry naturally.
  8. Put all used items in the yellow bag. Remove PPE. Clean your hands.
10% · 5000 ppm

100 ml of 5% bleach + 900 ml of water.

Do not shorten the 5-minute contact time. The chlorine needs this time to kill the germs.
  • Tell your supervisor about the spill.
Module 3 · 3.2 Spill kitGuidebook p. 112–113

Contents of the spill kit

Blood and body fluid spill kit

  • Keep a spill kit in each ward, the laboratory, the sample collection room, labour room and OT.
  • After use, fill the kit again immediately. Put it back in its usual place.
  • Write the date on the hypochlorite bottle. Replace it after 30 days.
  • Stored hypochlorite loses half of its strength in 30 days.
  • Get a mop and bucket from housekeeping. Wash and disinfect them after use.
Module 3 · Chemical spillsGuidebook p. 113–116

Chemical spill: five steps

  1. Make the area safe. Keep people away. Stand the container up, or put it in a larger container.
  2. Read the safety data sheet (MSDS). It tells you how to clean the chemical and how to give first aid.
  3. Clean the spill. Wear nitrile gloves and goggles. Cover the spill with absorbent from the edges in. Collect it with a dust pan into a labelled chemical bin.
  4. Tell your supervisor.
  5. Fill the chemical spill kit again.
  • Chemical that burns easily (for example, spirit): turn off all electrical and heat equipment first.
  • Acid: put sodium bicarbonate on it slowly, from the outside in.
  • Do not breathe the powder or the gas.
  • Do not use a vacuum cleaner on a chemical spill.
Module 3 · Chemical spillsGuidebook p. 67–70

Broken thermometer or BP apparatus: mercury spill

WarningMercury vapour is poisonous. You cannot see it or smell it. It causes damage to the brain, kidneys and unborn babies.
  1. Move all people away from the area. Open the windows.
  2. Remove your rings, watch and other jewellery.
  3. Put on a mask and rubber gloves.
  4. Pick up broken glass carefully. Put it in a puncture-proof container.
  5. Find the mercury beads with a torch. Push them together with a card.
  1. Pull up the beads with a syringe without a needle. Put them in a bottle half full of water. Close it tightly.
  2. Pick up very small beads with sticky tape.
  3. Seal all used items in a bag. Label it: "Hazardous Waste, Handle with Care".
  4. Fill the mercury spill kit again.
  • Do not use a broom or a vacuum cleaner. Do not touch mercury with bare hands. Do not pour mercury into a drain.

Infection prevention, control and staff safety

Hand hygiene. Personal protective equipment (PPE). Sharps and needle-stick injury.

40 minutes · 4.1 Hand hygiene · 4.2 PPE · 4.3 Sharps and NSI

Module 4 · 4.1 Hand hygieneGuidebook p. 83–88

Hand rub or hand wash?

Dirty hands are the main cause of infections in hospitals.

Soap and water · 40–60 seconds

  • When your hands are visibly dirty.
  • When there is blood or body fluid on your hands.
  • After you use the toilet.
  • During an outbreak of diarrhoea.

Do each step 5 times. Dry with clean paper. If the tap is not an elbow tap, close it with the paper.

Alcohol hand rub · 20–30 seconds

  • When your hands are not visibly dirty.
  • Before and after you touch a patient.
  • After you remove gloves.
  • After you touch objects near the patient.

Use enough to wet all of both hands. Rub until dry. Apply only to dry hands.

  • Do not use soap and hand rub at the same time.
  • Keep nails short. Remove rings, watches and bracelets.
Module 4 · 4.1 Hand hygiene · nurses and attendersGuidebook p. 87–88 (WHO)

Six steps to clean your hands

1Rub palm against palm.
2Put one palm on the back of the other hand. Interlace the fingers and rub. Change hands.
3Put palm against palm. Interlace the fingers and rub.
4Lock the fingers together. Rub the backs of the fingers against the other palm.
5Hold one thumb in the other hand. Rub it in a circle. Change hands.
6Rub the fingertips in a circle in the other palm. Change hands.
  • Use the same six steps for soap and water and for alcohol hand rub.
Module 4 · 4.1 Hand hygiene · staff nursesGuidebook p. 84–86, Figure 32 (WHO)

WHO five moments for hand hygiene

1

Before you touch a patient

Nurse: before pulse or BP. Attender: before you help a patient to move.

2

Before a clean or aseptic task

Nurse: before an injection, dressing or catheter. Attender: before mouth care or food.

3

After body fluid risk

Nurse: after you take blood or remove a dressing. Attender: after you clean urine or faeces. Also after you remove gloves.

4

After you touch a patient

Nurse: after an examination. Attender: after you help a patient to move.

5

After you touch the patient's area

Nurse: after you adjust the drip rate. Attender: after you change an empty bed. Do this also if you did not touch the patient.

Module 4 · 4.2 PPEGuidebook p. 96–101 · CDC sequence

Put on and remove PPE in the correct order

Donning (put on)

  1. Clean your hands.
  2. Put on the gown or apron. Tie it at the neck and waist.
  3. Put on the mask. Press the metal strip on your nose.
  4. Put on goggles or face shield.
  5. Put on gloves. Pull them over the cuffs of the gown.

Doffing (remove)

  1. Remove the gloves. Do not touch the outside with bare skin.
  2. Remove goggles or face shield by the strap.
  3. Remove the gown. Roll it inside out, away from your body.
  4. Remove the mask by the ties or ear loops. Do not touch the front.
  5. Clean your hands.
  • The outside of used PPE is dirty. Remove the dirtiest items first.
  • Wash utility gloves with soap and water before you remove them. Discard used disposable PPE in the correct bag.
Module 4 · 4.3 SharpsGuidebook p. 61–62

Sharps safety

Never recapDo not put the cap back on a used needle with two hands. Most needle-stick injuries occur during recapping.
  • Do not bend or break needles.
  • Do not give sharps to another person by hand.
  • Do not walk with sharps in your hand.
  • Do not leave sharps on beds, trays or floors.
  • Cut the needle with the needle cutter immediately after use.
  • Put sharps in the white container at the place of use.
  • Pick up broken glass with forceps, tongs or a brush and dust pan.
  • If a patient is agitated, get help before you use sharps.
Module 4 · 4.3 NSIGuidebook p. 63–64, Table 12

Needle-stick injury or splash: first aid now

Skin: cut or prick

  • Remove the gloves.
  • Wash the wound immediately with running water and soap.
  • Do not squeeze the wound.
  • Do not scrub.
  • Do not put the finger in your mouth.

Eye

  • Flush the eye immediately with clean water or normal saline.
  • Keep contact lenses in while you flush. Then remove them.
  • Do not put soap or disinfectant in the eye.

Mouth

  • Spit out the fluid immediately.
  • Rinse with water or saline. Spit again. Do this several times.
  • Do not use soap or disinfectant in the mouth.
Do not put bleach, alcohol, betadine or other antiseptics on the wound. Do not panic.
Module 4 · 4.3 NSIGuidebook p. 64–66

Report immediately: post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)

0 hoursDo first aid. Tell your supervisor.
2 hoursBest time to start PEP for HIV.
24 hoursStart Hepatitis B treatment before this time.
72 hoursAfter this time, HIV PEP does not work.
  1. Tell the ward sister or your supervisor immediately.
  2. Go to the casualty medical officer or the designated doctor.
  3. Tell the doctor which patient the needle or blood came from, if you know.
  4. The doctor examines the risk and decides about PEP.
  5. Fill in the needle-stick injury report form.
  6. Go to all follow-up visits and tests.
  • Not every injury needs medicine. The doctor decides.
  • Your test results stay confidential.
  • Report every injury, also a small one.

Module 5 · QuizDiscuss each question, then show the answer

Quick quiz

Question 1

Which hypochlorite strength do you use for a large blood spill?

10% (5000 ppm). Wait 5 minutes.

Question 2

Which three buckets do you use, in which order?

Detergent water, clean water (rinse), disinfectant.

Question 3

A needle pricks your finger. What do you do first?

Wash with running water and soap. Do not squeeze or scrub. Then report.

Question 4

In which direction do you clean a room?

Top to bottom, clean to dirty, inside to the door.

Question 5

When do you replace a waste bag?

When it is 3/4 full.

Question 6

Which PPE item do you remove first?

The gloves. Clean your hands at the end.

Question 7

Can you use the ICU mop in the corridor?

No. Critical areas and general areas have different mops.

Question 8

Within how many hours is HIV PEP best started?

Within 2 hours. It does not work after 72 hours.

SummaryQuestions and answers

Ten rules to remember

  1. Clean your hands at the five moments. Use the six steps.
  2. Clean top to bottom, clean to dirty, inside out.
  3. Use the three-bucket system. Never use a broom in patient areas.
  4. Measure bleach. 1% for surfaces, 10% for large spills.
  5. Wash, disinfect and dry mops every day.
  1. Separate waste at source: yellow, red, blue, white, black or green.
  2. Replace bags at 3/4 full. Tie, label and bar-code them.
  3. Large spill: cover, flood with 10%, wait 5 minutes.
  4. Never recap a needle with two hands.
  5. Needle-stick injury: wash, report, see the doctor within 2 hours.
If you are not sure about a rule, stop and ask your supervisor or the infection control nurse.
